# Chapter 27: A Glossary of Essential Terms

---

This chapter gathers the most essential Buddhist terms across traditions — Pali, Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and Japanese — organized thematically. This is a reference chapter; feel free to skip around or return to it as needed.

For a more complete alphabetical glossary including pronunciation guides, see the separate glossary document in this project.

---

## Foundational Concepts

**Dhamma / Dharma** (Pali/Sanskrit) — One of the most multivalent words in Buddhism. It means: (1) the teachings of the Buddha; (2) the truth of how things are; (3) phenomena in general — any experienced object or event. Context determines which meaning is active.

**Sangha** — the community of practitioners. Narrowly: the ordained monastic community. Broadly: all those who practice with sincerity.

**Nibbāna / Nirvāna** (Pali/Sanskrit) — liberation; the extinguishing of craving, hatred, and delusion; the cessation of suffering. Often mischaracterized as a kind of annihilation. More accurately: the end of the reactive momentum that perpetuates suffering.

**Samsāra** — the cycle of conditioned existence; the round of rebirth driven by karma and craving. Often described as beginningless: no first moment of ignorance can be identified.

**Dukkha** — suffering, unsatisfactoriness, the quality of incompleteness in conditioned experience. One of the three characteristics.

**Anicca** — impermanence. Everything that arises passes away. One of the three characteristics.

**Anattā / Anātman** (Pali/Sanskrit) — non-self. There is no fixed, permanent, independent self. One of the three characteristics.

## The Path

**Sīla** — ethics, moral conduct, the precepts. The foundation of the path.

**Samādhi** — concentration; meditative unification of mind; the cultivation of stable, clear, one-pointed attention.

**Pañña / Prajñā** (Pali/Sanskrit) — wisdom; direct insight into the nature of reality, especially the three characteristics and emptiness.

**Jhāna / Dhyāna** (Pali/Sanskrit) — absorption; deep states of meditative concentration in which the hindrances are temporarily subdued and the mind is unified.

**Viriya** — energy, effort, diligence on the path. One of the five spiritual faculties and one of the seven factors of awakening.

**Sati** — mindfulness; the quality of clear, present-moment awareness; remembering to come back. One of the factors of the Eightfold Path and the basis of Vipassanā practice.

**Samatha** — calm, tranquility, serenity. The stabilizing dimension of meditation, developed through concentration practices.

**Vipassanā** — insight; clear seeing; direct investigation of the three characteristics in experience.

---

## Mind and Psychology

**Citta** — heart-mind; the knowing, experiencing quality of consciousness. Includes both cognitive and emotional dimensions.

**Vedanā** — feeling-tone; the quality of pleasantness, unpleasantness, or neutrality that accompanies every moment of experience.

**Cetanā** — volition, intention. The aspect of mind that moves toward action. In the karmic framework, cetanā is what makes an action karmic.

**Manas** — the thinking, reasoning mind; the mental faculty that processes perceptions and generates concepts.

**Ālayavijñāna** — "storehouse consciousness" in Yogacara philosophy; the underlying stream of consciousness that carries karmic seeds from moment to moment and life to life.

**Kleśa** — mental afflictions; the disturbing emotions and cognitive distortions that obscure clarity and generate suffering. The three poisons (greed, hatred, delusion) are the root kleśas.

---

## Mahāyāna Terms

**Bodhicitta** — awakening mind; the aspiration to achieve full Buddhahood for the benefit of all sentient beings. The heart of the Mahāyāna path.

**Bodhisattva** — a being oriented toward awakening for others; one who has generated bodhicitta and is traveling the path toward full Buddhahood.

**Śūnyatā** — emptiness; the absence of inherent, independent existence in all phenomena.

**Tathāgata** — an epithet for a fully awakened being (Buddha). Means "one who has thus gone" or "one who has thus come."

**Tathāgatagarbha** — Buddha-nature; the potential for awakening that all beings inherently possess.

**Upāya** — skillful means; the ability to teach and act in ways that are appropriate to the conditions and needs of the situation.

**Pāramitā** — perfections; the qualities cultivated by a bodhisattva: generosity, ethics, patience, energy, meditation, wisdom (and sometimes four more: skillful means, aspiration, strength, primordial wisdom).

---

## Tibetan Terms

**Lama** — teacher; in Tibetan Buddhism, a qualified spiritual guide who has received proper training and transmission.

**Rinpoche** — "precious one"; an honorific for a recognized teacher, often but not always a tulku (reincarnate lama).

**Tulku** — a recognized reincarnation of a previous teacher; the Dalai Lama is the most well-known example.

**Empowerment (wang)** — initiation into a tantric practice; a transmission from teacher to student that creates the karmic connection and authorization for a specific practice.

**Ngöndro** — foundational/preliminary practices; the four preliminary practices done 100,000 times each as preparation for advanced tantric practice.

**Rigpa** — in Dzogchen, the awareness that is the true nature of mind; pure, luminous, open presence.

**Bardo** — intermediate state; the period between death and rebirth, described in detail in the Tibetan tradition.

---

## Zen / Chan Terms

**Zazen** — seated meditation; the core practice of Zen.

**Koan** — a question or statement used in Rinzai Zen practice to cut through conceptual thinking.

**Dokusan / Sanzen** — private interview between student and teacher in which the student presents their understanding.

**Sesshin** — intensive Zen retreat, typically five to seven days.

**Shikantaza** — "just sitting"; the Soto Zen practice of pure, objectless sitting presence.

**Satori / Kenshō** — insight; a moment of awakening or seeing one's nature. Often described as a sudden opening, though the tradition distinguishes between brief initial glimpses (kenshō) and the deeper, more stable realization that follows sustained practice.

**Mu** — "no" or "nothing"; used as a koan object in the fundamental koan of Zhaozhou's dog. Working with Mu until all conceptual grasping is exhausted.

---

## Japanese Buddhist Terms

**Nembutsu** — the recitation of Amitābha Buddha's name; the primary practice of Pure Land Buddhism.

**Amida** — the Japanese name for Amitābha, the Buddha of Infinite Light, central to Pure Land practice.

**Jōdo** — Pure Land; the buddha-field of Amitābha.

**Tariki / Jiriki** — other-power / self-power; the distinction between practice that relies on Amitābha's vow (tariki) and practice that relies on one's own effort (jiriki).
