# Chapter 36: A Directory of Resources

---

This chapter is a practical guide to finding teachers, centers, retreats, books, and online resources. It is not exhaustive — the Buddhist world is vast, and any list here reflects particular emphases. Consider it a starting point, not a complete map.

---

## Practice Centers and Organizations

### Theravāda / Insight Meditation

**Insight Meditation Society (IMS)** — Barre, Massachusetts. One of the founding institutions of Western insight meditation. Offers retreats year-round, including a rigorous annual three-month retreat. ims.dharma.org

**Spirit Rock Meditation Center** — Woodacre, California. The West Coast counterpart to IMS, with a similar lineage and a particularly strong emphasis on socially engaged practice. spiritrock.org

**Gaia House** — Devon, United Kingdom. The primary European insight meditation retreat center, connected to the same lineage. gaiahouse.co.uk

**Dhamma.org (S.N. Goenka centers)** — A global network of Vipassanā centers offering the ten-day Goenka retreat, which is offered by donation and widely available. dhamma.org

### Zen

**San Francisco Zen Center** — San Francisco, California. Founded by Shunryu Suzuki Roshi. Three practice centers including Tassajara, the first Soto Zen monastery outside Asia. sfzc.org

**Zen Mountain Monastery** — Mt. Tremper, New York. A Rinzai/Soto Zen training monastery with a strong emphasis on the arts and engaged practice. mro.org

**Plum Village** — Dordogne, France (and global affiliates). Founded by Thich Nhat Hanh. Engaged Buddhist practice integrating mindfulness into all aspects of daily life. plumvillage.org

### Tibetan Buddhism

**Shambhala** — A global network founded by Chögyam Trungpa Rinpoche, emphasizing the basic goodness of human beings and secular application of Buddhist principles. shambhala.org

**Rigpa** — An international network of Buddhist study and practice centers founded by Sogyal Rinpoche. Access to Nyingma teachings including Dzogchen. rigpa.org

**Naropa University** — Boulder, Colorado. The first Buddhist-inspired university in North America. Offers degree programs in contemplative education. naropa.edu

**Kagyu Samye Ling** — Scotland. The first Tibetan Buddhist monastery in the West. eskdalemuir.com/samye-ling

## Books: A Selective Reading List

### Starting Points

- *In the Buddha's Words* edited by Bhikkhu Bodhi — A comprehensive anthology of early suttas organized thematically. The best single introduction to the Pali Canon.
- *What the Buddha Taught* by Walpola Rahula — A concise, lucid introduction to early Buddhist thought by a Sri Lankan monk.
- *The Heart of the Buddha's Teaching* by Thich Nhat Hanh — A warm, accessible introduction drawing primarily on Theravāda teachings.
- *Buddhism Without Beliefs* by Stephen Batchelor — A thoughtful secular interpretation of the core teachings.

### Meditation Practice

- *Mindfulness in Plain English* by Bhante Gunaratana — The clearest available instruction in basic Vipassanā practice. Available free online.
- *The Mind Illuminated* by Culadasa (John Yates) — A detailed, comprehensive meditation manual integrating Theravāda practice with cognitive science. Demanding but rewarding.
- *Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind* by Shunryu Suzuki — Essential reading on Zen practice. Accessible, profound, quietly inexhaustible.
- *Waking Up* by Sam Harris — A secular but serious engagement with meditation practice and the question of consciousness.

### Loving-Kindness and Compassion

- *Lovingkindness: The Revolutionary Art of Happiness* by Sharon Salzberg — The standard Western text on mettā practice.
- *The Places That Scare You* by Pema Chödrön — Practical instruction in Tibetan compassion practices including tonglen. One of the best Buddhist books for people in genuine difficulty.

### Mahāyāna and Tibetan

- *The Way of the Bodhisattva* (Bodhicaryavatara) by Shantideva — An 8th-century masterwork on the bodhisattva path. Both a philosophical text and a meditation practice. Get the Crosby and Skilton or Padmakara translation.
- *The Tibetan Book of Living and Dying* by Sogyal Rinpoche — Accessible introduction to Tibetan Buddhist teachings on death and practice.
- *Words of My Perfect Teacher* by Patrul Rinpoche — A comprehensive, often funny, always profound guide to the Nyingma preliminary practices.

### Buddhist Psychology and Neuroscience

- *Altered Traits* by Daniel Goleman and Richard Davidson — The most rigorous scientific assessment of meditation research to date.
- *The Embodied Mind* by Varela, Thompson, and Rosch — The foundational text on the dialogue between cognitive science and Buddhist philosophy.
- *Trauma-Sensitive Mindfulness* by David Treleaven — Essential for anyone teaching meditation.

### History and Philosophy

- *Buddhist Religions: A Historical Introduction* by Richard Robinson et al. — The standard academic survey.
- *In Search of the Buddha* by Charles Allen — A readable account of the 19th-century rediscovery of Buddhism's history in India.
- *Why Buddhism Is True* by Robert Wright — An engaging if sometimes reductive attempt to read Buddhist teachings through the lens of evolutionary psychology.

## Teachers

The following teachers have established significant bodies of work accessible to English-speaking practitioners. This list is necessarily partial.

**Theravāda tradition:** Ajahn Chah (1918–1992, extensive talks available), Ajahn Sumedho, Joseph Goldstein, Sharon Salzberg, Jack Kornfield, Bhikkhu Bodhi, Thanissaro Bhikkhu, Bhante Gunaratana

**Zen tradition:** Shunryu Suzuki (1904–1971), Thich Nhat Hanh (1926–2022), Robert Aitken (1917–2010), Taizan Maezumi (1931–1995), Bernie Glassman (1939–2018), Joan Halifax, John Daido Loori (1931–2009)

**Tibetan tradition:** The 14th Dalai Lama, Pema Chödrön, Chögyam Trungpa (1939–1987), Tsoknyi Rinpoche, Mingyur Rinpoche, Ajahn Brahm (influenced by both Theravāda and Tibetan traditions), Adyashanti (non-sectarian but Zen-rooted)

## Online Resources

**Dharma Seed** (dharmaseed.org) — Thousands of free talks from teachers in the insight meditation tradition.

**Tricycle** (tricycle.org) — Independent Buddhist magazine with extensive free online content.

**Lion's Roar** (lionsroar.com) — Buddhist magazine with strong coverage of multiple traditions.

**Sutta Central** (suttacentral.net) — Free access to the entire Pali Canon in multiple translations, along with parallel texts from other early Buddhist traditions.

**Access to Insight** (accesstoinsight.org) — A comprehensive library of Theravāda texts and practice guides.

**YouTube** — Many teachers and centers maintain active YouTube channels. Search for talks by the teachers listed above.

---

## Apps

**Insight Timer** — Largest free meditation app, with guided meditations from thousands of teachers across traditions, a timer, and community features.

**Waking Up** (Sam Harris) — Secular but rigorous meditation instruction with courses and talks. Subscription model with generous scholarship policy.

**Ten Percent Happier** — More beginner-friendly, with courses and guided meditations from a range of Buddhist and secular teachers.

**Plum Village app** — Free guided meditations in the tradition of Thich Nhat Hanh.

## A Note on Starting

The resources in this chapter can become their own obstacle. Reading about Buddhism is not practicing Buddhism. The most important step is the simplest one: sit down, set a timer for fifteen minutes, and bring your attention to the breath.

The tradition has been offering this invitation for 2,500 years. It's still good.
